# Sampler & Scheduler Behavior for SDXL-Turbo: The Few-Step Special Case of SDXL Sampling

> [`SDXL-Turbo_Research_Verification.md`](./SDXL-Turbo_Research_Verification.md); read that

> doesn't apply, and the four defects it surfaced and fixed. The body below is left as-is.

## TL;DR
- **SDXL-Turbo collapses the entire multi-step sampler/scheduler design space into three mandatory settings — EulerAncestralDiscreteScheduler with `timestep_spacing="trailing"`, `num_inference_steps=1–4`, and `guidance_scale=0.0` — because Adversarial Diffusion Distillation (ADD) trained the UNet to jump directly to the data manifold at a handful of fixed timesteps starting from t≈999.** High-order solvers (DPM++, UniPC, Heun), Karras/AYS sigma schedules, CFG scale, negative prompts, and `guidance_rescale` are all dead knobs; the only meaningful ones are steps (1–4), img2img strength, noise-seed policy, and resolution (native 512×512).
- For **commercial** real-time V2V products, SDXL-Turbo's Stability AI Community License is a live constraint (free access is preserved only for people/organizations generating annual revenue **less than US $1,000,000**, and the license terminates above that), so Lightning (OpenRAIL++), LCM/LCM-LoRA, TCD, and Hyper-SD are the safer bases; each has its own scheduler contract (Lightning→Euler+sgm_uniform/trailing, LCM→LCMScheduler, TCD→TCDScheduler with a gamma knob, Hyper-SD→TCD or Euler-a).
- In StreamDiffusion-class pipelines the scheduler is effectively rebuilt by hand (LCMScheduler used only for constants, manual `alpha_prod/beta_prod` add-noise + a c_skip/c_out consistency step), CFG is off (`cfg_type="none"`), and **temporal flicker is controlled primarily by reusing a single fixed noise tensor across frames** rather than by sampler choice.

## Key Findings

**1. ADD constrains the sampler because the student is a fixed-timestep noise→data map, not an ODE integrand.** ADD (Sauer, Lorenz, Blattmann, Rombach; arXiv 2311.17042, published Nov 28 2023, distilled from SDXL-Base-1.0) trains the student to sample `s` uniformly from a small set of N=4 chosen student timesteps `T_student = {τ₁,…,τ_N}`, with `τ_N = 1000` and **zero-terminal-SNR enforced** "as the model needs to start from pure noise during inference." The student outputs an on-manifold x̂θ in a single forward pass, supervised by an adversarial (hinge) loss with a DINOv2 ViT-S discriminator plus a score-distillation loss against a frozen SDXL teacher. Because the model is only optimized at those discrete noise levels, generic multi-step ODE/SDE solvers that assume a smooth score field along a continuous trajectory have nothing correct to integrate — they either do nothing useful or push the latent off-manifold.

**2. Trailing spacing is mandatory and directly inherits the Lin et al. terminal-SNR argument.** SDXL-Turbo must "see t=999 first." `timestep_spacing="trailing"` (from Lin et al., "Common Diffusion Noise Schedules and Sample Steps Are Flawed," WACV 2024, arXiv 2305.08891, Table 2) is the only spacing that includes the last timestep and steps backward from it. Leading/linspace spacing skips the terminal step, so the model receives an input that does not match the pure-noise, zero-terminal-SNR distribution it was trained on — producing the well-known washed-out/blurry failure. The diffusers docs codify this: for `from_single_file` loading you must set `EulerAncestralDiscreteScheduler.from_config(..., timestep_spacing="trailing")`.

**3. CFG must be 0 and negative prompts are inert.** ADD explicitly "does not use classifier-free guidance during inference, further reducing memory requirements." The model was never trained to be steered by a guidance vector, so enabling CFG>1 pushes latents into an oversaturated/"burnt" regime (community consensus, per Stable Diffusion Art's ComfyUI testing: images brighten and saturate just above CFG 1, and degrade below 1). At CFG=1 the unconditional and conditional predictions collapse, so **a negative prompt "doesn't change a pixel."** `guidance_rescale` (the Lin et al. over-exposure fix) is therefore also moot.

**4. Native resolution is 512×512 despite SDXL architecture** — a direct consequence of ADD's pixel-space discriminator. As the follow-up LADD/SD3-Turbo paper (Sauer et al., "Fast High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Adversarial Diffusion Distillation," SIGGRAPH Asia 2024) explains, "the usage of the fixed and pretrained DINOv2 network restricts the discriminator's training resolution to 518 × 518 pixels... ADD needs to decode to RGB space, as the discriminator has not been trained in latent space, which significantly hinders high-resolution training > 512² pixels." Larger sizes "work as well" per the model card but were not the training target and degrade coherence.

**5. Quality plateaus at ~4 steps and degrades at 5–10.** Increasing steps 1→2→4 improves consistency and detail (ADD's own COCO FID5k: 20.6 at 1 step, 20.8 at 2, 20.3 at 4), but beyond ~4 steps the Turbo noise schedule (near-linear sigma drop, unlike Karras) means the denoiser is asked to estimate noise levels it wasn't distilled for, so quality visibly degrades at 5–10 steps.

## Details

### The distillation trajectory and why high-order solvers are pointless
ADD's forward process is the standard `x_s = α_s·x₀ + σ_s·ε` with the student's own α/σ coefficients, but training only ever visits N=4 timesteps. The one-step teacher signal is sufficient (ADD Table 1f: 1 teacher step gives FID 20.6, equal to 4). Multi-step inference in Turbo is implemented as **denoise → renoise → denoise**: after a partial x₀ prediction, fresh noise is re-added to reach the next distilled timestep. The SDXL-Lightning paper (Lin, Wang, Yang; arXiv 2402.13929) states this plainly about ADD: "since the quality for one-step inference is not good enough, random noises are added again for multi-step inference. This way of multi-step inference significantly alters the model behavior, making it less compatible with existing LoRA modules and control plugins." This is why:
- **DPM++/UniPC/Heun (high-order ODE) solvers are pointless or harmful**: they presume a well-defined score along a continuous PF-ODE. Turbo's student is a direct x₀ predictor at 4 discrete points; multistep linear-multistep/predictor-corrector machinery has no valid intermediate trajectory to exploit.
- **Karras/exponential/AYS sigma schedules do not apply**: the Turbo noise schedule drops sigma roughly linearly with step, structurally unlike the front-loaded Karras curve. In practice (per Stable Diffusion Art's ComfyUI testing) Euler-a with the default Karras schedule yields a "similar result" at 1–4 steps only because so few steps are taken that the schedule shape barely matters — not because Karras is doing anything principled.

### Exact configurable surface
Official diffusers text-to-image path:
```python
pipe = AutoPipelineForText2Image.from_pretrained("stabilityai/sdxl-turbo",
        torch_dtype=torch.float16, variant="fp16").to("cuda")
image = pipe(prompt, num_inference_steps=1, guidance_scale=0.0).images[0]
```
- **Scheduler**: EulerAncestralDiscreteScheduler, `timestep_spacing="trailing"`. Euler-a's ancestral renoising matches the ADD renoise-between-steps assumption; plain Euler (deterministic) also works but discards the stochastic renoising the student expects at multi-step. ComfyUI exposes this as the **SDTurboScheduler** node (class `SDTurboScheduler`, category `sampling/custom_sampling/schedulers`), which generates the sigma sequence from the model given `steps` and a `denoise` parameter (denoise shifts the starting sigma, used for img2img). Community-workable `sampler_name` choices at 1–4 steps: `euler_ancestral` (default), `euler`, and `dpmpp_sde`/`dpmpp_2s_ancestral` variants (the Inference-Time Distillation paper, arXiv 2412.08871, even benchmarks Turbo with 4–6-step DPM++ 2S Ancestral on a DreamShaper community checkpoint). High-order deterministic multistep samplers (dpmpp_2m, uni_pc) are not recommended.
- **Steps**: 1 is enough; 2–4 improves quality; >4 degrades.
- **guidance_scale**: 0.0.

### Stochasticity at 1–4 steps
Ancestral noise injection means something different here than in 50-step SDXL. In multi-step SDXL, ancestral eta mostly adds texture diversity. In Turbo, the **renoising between the 1–4 distilled steps is part of the student's expected input distribution** — the model was trained expecting to be re-noised to the next τ. Deterministic Euler skips that injection and can look flatter; Euler-a supplies it. Reproducibility implications: at **1 step**, output is a deterministic function of the initial noise seed (no ancestral step follows), so seed fully determines the image and seed sweeps are the only exploration knob. At **2–4 steps**, each ancestral renoise consumes additional randomness, so the same base seed yields more variation across step counts, though ADD notes "the general layout is preserved across sampling steps" for a fixed seed (enabling fast seed exploration at 1 step, then refinement).

### img2img / V2V mechanics
The binding constraint is `num_inference_steps * strength ≥ 1`, because the pipeline runs `int(num_inference_steps * strength)` steps (e.g. strength 0.5 × 2 steps = 1 step). With only 1–4 distilled timesteps, `strength` selects **which** τ the input latent is noised to and how many of the remaining distilled steps run — it is timestep truncation, not a continuous denoise fraction. Real-time V2V code (e.g. radames' Real-Time-Latent-Consistency-Model space) guards this with `if int(steps*strength) < 1: steps = math.ceil(1/max(0.10, strength))`. Practical V2V sweet spots: low strength (~0.3–0.5, i.e. 1 effective step) preserves the input structure for stable frame-to-frame coherence; higher strength (→1.0) allows more prompt-driven change but increases flicker.

### StreamDiffusion internals (Kodaira et al., arXiv 2312.12491, ICCV 2025)
StreamDiffusion does **not** run a stock LCM/diffusers sampling loop. In `pipeline.py`:
- It instantiates `LCMScheduler.from_config(...)` only to borrow constants (`alphas_cumprod`, boundary-condition scalings), then in `prepare()` sets a 50-step schedule and indexes into it with `t_index_list` — so `t_index_list=[32,45]` picks the 32nd/45th entries (two noise levels), `[0,16,32,45]` picks four. It precomputes `self.alpha_prod_t_sqrt` and `self.beta_prod_t_sqrt` per substep.
- **add_noise** is manual: `alpha_prod_t_sqrt[i]·x₀ + beta_prod_t_sqrt[i]·noise`.
- **denoise** is a manual LCM-consistency step: `F_theta = (x_t − beta·model_pred)/alpha; denoised = c_out·F_theta + c_skip·x_t`. For SD-Turbo there is a dedicated `txt2img_sd_turbo()` doing a single raw x₀ prediction `(x_t − beta·model_pred)/alpha` with no c_skip/c_out.
- **RCFG**: `cfg_type ∈ {none, full, self, initialize}`, `delta=1.0`, `guidance_scale=1.2` default. `none` = N UNet passes (forces guidance_scale=1.0); `full` = true CFG at 2N cost; `self` (Self-Negative RCFG) approximates the uncond term as `stock_noise·delta` at **N** cost; `initialize` (Onetime-Negative) computes the negative once at N+1 cost. The paper reports RCFG "leads to up-to 2.05× speedups" vs conventional CFG (2.05× Self-Negative / 1.79× Onetime-Negative, measured at denoising step 5) and reaches "up-to 91.07 fps on one RTX 4090 GPU, improving the throughputs of AutoPipeline developed by Diffusers over 59.56×."
- **Turbo interaction with RCFG**: Because Turbo is CFG-free, StreamDiffusion recommends `cfg_type="none"` for text2img ("You cannot use anything other than 'none' for txt2img mode"), and img2img examples enforce `if guidance_scale <= 1.0: cfg_type="none"`. RCFG only activates when you deliberately set guidance_scale>1.0 in img2img to push away from the input latent; `delta` softens its effect. So RCFG is largely orthogonal to pure-Turbo operation.
- **Flicker mitigation**: the dominant control is a **single fixed `init_noise` tensor drawn once (seed default 2) and reused every frame** — identical inputs then map to identical outputs, killing flicker. dotsimulate's TouchDesigner build notes the inverse: seed = −1 (random per frame) intentionally creates flicker. Additional: the **Stochastic Similarity Filter** (`enable_similar_image_filter(threshold=0.98, max_skip_frame=10)`) probabilistically skips UNet/VAE on near-duplicate frames — a power-saving feature (on a 1000-frame dynamic scene on one RTX 4090 it cuts average power "from 236.13w to 199.38w," and on a static-scene input from 238.68w→119.77w on the 4090 and 85.96w→35.91w on an RTX 3060) — and **TAESD/taesdxl** tiny VAE for latency. Note: the base cumulo-autumn repo targets SD1.5/SD2.1 (SD-Turbo); first-class SDXL-Turbo support (dual text encoders, taesdxl, 2048-dim embeds, TensorRT) lives in forks (daydreamlive, dotsimulate/StreamDiffusionTD, hkn-g sdxl branch).

### Empirical evidence and quality signatures
- **ADD human-preference**: ADD-XL at a **single step** beats LCM-XL (1/2/4-step), StyleGAN-T, InstaFlow and OpenMUSE in both image quality and prompt alignment, losing only to full SDXL at 1 step; at **4 steps ADD-XL outperforms its teacher SDXL-Base-1.0** (no refiner) at 512². FID/CLIP caveats: all evaluated at 512², and the authors (and later Hyper-SD/TLCM papers) note **COCO FID is unreliable** for these models, so preference studies dominate.
- **Cross-method**: SDXL-Lightning (adversarial + progressive distillation, latent-space discriminator) is generally rated higher-fidelity than Turbo at matched steps and is not resolution-capped to 512; its 1-step `x0`-pred checkpoint is "experimental," 2-step recommended as the floor. Hyper-SD (ByteDance) is reported quantitatively better than Lightning at 1–2 steps and ships CFG-preserved 8/12-step LoRAs. TCD adds a gamma/eta stochasticity knob and can exceed the teacher at high NFE. All distilled models share Turbo's signatures: **reduced diversity/mode coverage, oversharpened "processed"/oversaturated look at 4 steps, weak legible-text rendering, and degraded anatomy/faces** (worsened at 512 vs upscaled). SDXL-Turbo's own model card lists lower sample diversity than SDXL and unreliable text/faces.
- **Latency**: ADD reports <1 s single-step on A100/H100. StreamDiffusion on RTX 4090 + TensorRT + TAESD: SD-Turbo 1-step ≈ 106 fps txt2img / 93.9 fps img2img; LCM-LoRA 4-step ≈ 38 fps. Turbo/Lightning ~0.3 s at 1–2 steps on a 4090; INT8 for SDXL UNet in TensorRT can be slower than FP16 in some configs (a documented NVIDIA/TensorRT issue), so FP16 is the safer real-time default.

### 2025–2026 developments
Newer real-time distillation has largely superseded ADD for quality: **DMD2** (Improved Distribution Matching Distillation, arXiv 2405.14867), **NitroFusion/NitroSD** (Dar-Yen Chen, Hmrishav Bandyopadhyay, Kai Zou, Yi-Zhe Song; University of Surrey; arXiv 2412.02030, Dec 2 2024), whose authors report it "significantly outperforms existing single-step methods across multiple evaluation metrics, particularly excelling in preserving fine details and global consistency," benchmarking NitroSD-Realism/-Vibrant against SDXL-Turbo and SDXL-Lightning 1-step baselines; plus TCD, Hyper-SD, and flow-matching Turbo variants (e.g., Z-Image-Turbo, 8-step, with dedicated rectified-flow samplers). DMD/DMD2-distilled models are "incompatible with conventional solvers" and use iterative random sampling, reinforcing the general rule that distilled few-step models each demand their own scheduler.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — If you can accept the research/limited-commercial license and want maximum speed:** Use SDXL-Turbo (or SD-Turbo for SD2.1-class speed) with EulerAncestralDiscreteScheduler, `timestep_spacing="trailing"`, `guidance_scale=0.0`, 1 step for pure real-time V2V, 2–4 steps when you can afford the latency for detail. Native 512×512; upscale with a tiny/cheap post pass rather than generating larger. Use TAESD/taesdxl and TensorRT FP16.

**Stage 2 — For V2V temporal stability:** Fix the noise seed across frames (StreamDiffusion `init_noise` reuse); use low img2img strength (~0.3–0.5, 1 effective step) to lock structure; add cross-frame noise correlation and, if needed, TemporalNet/StreamV2V-style cached attention (in forks). Enable the stochastic similarity filter for power/thermal headroom, not quality.

**Stage 3 — If the product is commercial and revenue may exceed the license ceiling, switch base model:** Prefer **SDXL-Lightning** (OpenRAIL++) as the drop-in higher-quality replacement, or **LCM/LCM-LoRA** / **Hyper-SD** / **TCD** for LoRA-portable acceleration. Apply each method's scheduler contract exactly (table below).

**Benchmarks that change the decision:** If you observe washed-out/low-contrast output → your spacing is not trailing. If output is oversaturated/burnt → CFG is >0/>1; set it to 0 (Turbo) or into the method's narrow band. If quality worsens as you add steps past 4 → you are outside the distilled timestep set; drop back to ≤4. If per-frame flicker is the blocker → move seed policy to fixed before touching the sampler.

### Compact scheduler/CFG contract per method
| Method | Scheduler | Spacing / special | Steps | CFG | Key knob |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SDXL-Turbo (ADD) | EulerAncestralDiscrete | trailing (mandatory) | 1–4 | 0.0 | steps, strength, seed |
| SDXL-Lightning | EulerDiscrete | sgm_uniform (ComfyUI) / trailing; 1-step uses `prediction_type="sample"` (x0) | 1/2/4/8 (match ckpt) | ~0–1 (≈1) | correct ckpt per step |
| LCM / LCM-LoRA | LCMScheduler | — | 2–8 | 1.0–2.0 | guidance in [1,2] |
| TCD | TCDScheduler | — | 1–8 (flexible NFE) | 0 | eta/gamma (0=det, 1=stochastic) |
| Hyper-SD | TCDScheduler (unified LoRA) or Euler-a | fuse LoRA ~0.125 scale | 1/2/4/8 | 0 (CFG-preserved 8/12-step LoRAs support 5–8) | eta; ckpt per step |

### Live vs dead knobs for a Turbo pipeline engineer
- **Live**: `num_inference_steps` (1–4), img2img `strength` (≥1/steps), **noise seed policy** (fixed for V2V), resolution (512² native).
- **Dead**: CFG scale (must be 0), sampler choice among high-order solvers (DPM++/UniPC/Heun), Karras/exponential/AYS sigma schedules, negative prompts, `guidance_rescale`.

## Caveats
- The "Karras works about the same" observation at 1–4 steps is community folklore (Stable Diffusion Art), true only because too few steps are taken for schedule shape to matter — do not extrapolate to higher steps.
- FID/CLIP numbers for all these models are widely acknowledged (by ADD, Hyper-SD, TLCM authors) as unreliable at 512² on COCO; treat human-preference results as primary and single-number benchmarks as directional.
- Latency figures are hardware/config specific; StreamDiffusion's 100+ fps requires TensorRT + TAESD + batched denoising and reflects SD-Turbo (SD2.1-class), not full SDXL-Turbo, whose SDXL UNet is heavier.
- SDXL-Turbo licensing (Stability AI Community License, LICENSE.md): free access is "preserved... for people or organizations generating annual revenue of less than US $1,000,000," and the license terminates if you or your affiliates "generate more than USD $1,000,000 in annual revenue" from covered use, requiring a paid Enterprise/membership tier. Verify current terms directly, as Stability has revised its license structure over time (the current Community License dates to July 5 2024).
- Base StreamDiffusion's SDXL-Turbo support is fork-dependent; validate the specific fork's scheduler handling before committing.
